Metrics and CBT models

How a test record becomes a set of metrics for every cycle, and how the analyst models turn those metrics into a CBT distribution.

The method is described in full in Sahin et al. (in preparation); see Citation and license. The code is in py/cbt_core.py, which runs in the browser.

Processing and cycle detection

  1. Read and convert. Strain given as a decimal is multiplied by 100. The excess pore pressure ratio is ru = (σ′v0 − σ′v)/σ′v0, with σ′v0 from the first row.
  2. Resample. Stress, strain and vertical stress histories are interpolated in time with a monotonic piecewise cubic Hermite spline (SciPy PchipInterpolator), at about 1,000 points per cycle, so every cycle is described at the same resolution whatever the recording rate.
  3. Detect cycles. A cycle starts where the shear stress crosses zero from negative to positive. Metrics are computed for every cycle.

Metrics

Maximum excess pore pressure ratio, ru,max

The largest ru within the cycle. Sand-like soils approach ru = 1 (nearly zero effective stress); clay-like soils typically level off well below it.

Hull ratio, Ratiohull

Ratiohull = Aloop / Ahull

Aloop is the area enclosed by the cycle's τ–γ loop and Ahull the area of its convex hull (the orange outline in the loop plot). A broad, elliptical loop nearly fills its hull (Ratiohull close to 1); a pinched, banana-shaped loop, typical of sand-like behavior with dilation and phase transformation in each cycle, fills less of it. Ratiohull is limited to 1.

Aloop is integrated with Simpson's rule. If the Simpson ratio is negative, exceeds 1 while the trapezoid ratio does not, or differs from the trapezoid ratio by more than 0.02, the trapezoid rule is used for that cycle instead: Simpson's rule can fail when the smoothed strain has zero or near-zero steps, as in coarsely quantized records. The rule used is reported per cycle (hull_method in the results).

Cyclic strain increment ratio, RatioΔγ

RatioΔγ = Δγcyc / γFC

Δγcyc is the increase in the maximum single-amplitude strain γSA from the previous cycle, and γFC is γSA of the first cycle. Large values mean strain grows quickly from cycle to cycle, as in sand-like soils after liquefaction is triggered.

Strain accumulation curvature, κγ

One value for the whole record: the three-point curvature of the curve γSA/γs against N/Ns, using points at 10%, 50% and 90% of the record, where γs = 3% and Ns is the (interpolated) number of cycles to reach γSA = 3%. Positive values mean strain accumulation accelerates, typical of sand-like behavior; values near zero or negative mean steady or decelerating accumulation.

If γSA = 3% is not reached, Ns is taken as the last cycle and strain is normalized by γSA at the end of the last cycle, γSA,last, so the curve ends at (1, 1); the three points are taken on that curve.

Analyst models

For each of the ten analysts, the logit of the CBT they assigned was regressed on a subset of the metrics with Bayesian inference (PyMC, 4 chains × 1,000 posterior samples):

μT = log[ CBT / (1 − CBT) ] = θ · X + σ̂ ε,   ε ~ N(0, 1)

where X holds the metrics used by that analyst's model (listed in Analyst models) and σ̂ is the model standard deviation. The posterior samples are stored in data/models.json.

For a new test and cycle, every posterior sample s of analyst i gives μs = θs · [1, X]. The analyst's predictive distribution of logit(CBT) is normal, with

μi = mean(μs)     σi² = var(μs) + mean(σ̂s²)

The first term of σi² is the uncertainty of the coefficients (σ coef.) and the second the model's own scatter (σ̂ model). Using every posterior sample keeps the correlation between the intercept and the coefficients.

Combining analysts

The selected analysts are combined in logit space with equal weights wi (Brandenberg and Pretell, 2024):

μc = Σ wi μi     σc² = Σ wi σi² + Σ wi (μi − μc)²

The first term is the within-analyst uncertainty and the second the between-analyst (epistemic) uncertainty. In the tool, μc and σc are shown as μT and σT. The result is transformed back to CBT with expit(x) = 1/(1 + e−x): the median is expit(μT), the percentiles are expit(μT + zσT), and the mean is integrated with Gauss–Hermite quadrature.

Calibration data

The models were fitted to 195 CDSS tests from the NGL database: stress-controlled tests on intact fine-grained specimens (fines content above 30%) with no initial static shear stress, carried to γDA ≥ 9%. The posterior means and standard deviations of the coefficients in data/models.json reproduce Table 2 of the paper. tools/build_models.py rebuilds the file from the Bayesian results.
