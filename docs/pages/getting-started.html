Your first test

In this tutorial you'll load one of the example tests, read its CBT, compare two cycles, and download the results. It takes about five minutes.

Step 1: Open the tool

Open the CBT Tool in a recent version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. While the calculation engine starts, a progress bar shows Starting the calculation engine… The first visit downloads about 30 MB (Python, NumPy and SciPy for the browser); later visits load from the browser's cache in a few seconds.

When it's ready, the Example buttons become active. The page has four boxes across the top:

  1. Prepare your data: the CSV template, three example tests, and the units of the file.
  2. Upload test files: drop CSV files here, or load tests from the NGL database. Each loaded test is listed with its CBT.
  3. Analyst models: the ten analyst models that are combined into one CBT estimate. All are ticked by default.
  4. Export: download the results or the raw data of the tests you pick.

Step 2: Load Example 1

Click Example 1. The tool sets the units for this file (time in minutes, stress in psf, strain as a decimal), computes the metrics and CBT for every cycle, and shows the test. This takes a second or two.

Example 1 is a CDSS test with 211 loading cycles at a cyclic stress ratio of about 0.39 and σ′v0 = 2611 psf. It reaches γDA = 9% in cycle 126 and ends at γDA = 11.48%.

Step 3: Read the result

The left column shows the test and the selected cycle. By default this is the last cycle, 211:

  • The summary at the top lists the number of cycles, σ′v0, CSR, the largest γDA, the first cycle with γDA ≥ 9%, and Ns, the number of cycles to reach a single-amplitude strain of 3%.
  • Combined CBT is 0.13: the test is clay-like. The gauge shows the median (dark line) and the 16th–84th percentile range (grey band), here 0.03 to 0.45. The table below gives the median, mean, percentiles, and the mean and standard deviation in logit space (μT, σT), split into within- and between-analyst parts.
  • Metrics are the four predictors used by the models at this cycle. Here the maximum excess pore pressure ratio is only 0.57 after 211 cycles, and the strain accumulation curvature κγ is close to zero (strain accumulates steadily, not at an accelerating rate). Both point to clay-like behavior.

Hover over any value to see a short description, or click ⓘ Symbols for the list of symbols.

Step 4: Look at another cycle

CBT is computed for every cycle, so you can see how it changes during the test. Use the slider, the ◀ ▶ buttons, or type a cycle number. Click First γDA ≥ 9% to jump to cycle 126:

Step 5: The plots

The right side has four tabs:

  • Test record: the stress–strain loops with the convex hull of the selected cycle, the stress path, the strain and pore pressure histories, and the strain accumulation curve used for κγ.
  • Metrics vs. cycle: how ru,max, Ratiohull, RatioΔγ and γDA develop over the test.
  • CBT: CBT of each analyst and the combined median against γDA, and the probability distribution of CBT at the selected cycle.
  • Analyst results: a table of each analyst model's estimate at the selected cycle.

Reading the results explains each plot.

Step 6: Compare with a sand-like test

Click Example 3. It is added to the file list under Example 1; click either name to switch between them. Example 3 reaches ru,max = 0.87, its loops pinch (Ratiohull = 0.84 at the last cycle) and its strain accumulation accelerates (κγ = 1.06). Its combined CBT at the last cycle is 0.80: sand-like.

Example 2 shows what happens when a test stops before γDA = 9%: CBT is still reported, with a yellow caution (see The γDA = 9% caution).

Step 7: Download

In the Export box, the dropdown lists the loaded tests, all ticked. Click Results to download a .zip with one CSV file of per-cycle metrics and CBT for each test, a summary table (when more than one test is ticked), and a disclaimer. Downloads describes the columns.
