Cyclic Behavior Type (CBT) Tool

Estimate whether a fine-grained soil responds to cyclic loading more like a sand or more like a clay, from the record of a cyclic direct simple shear (CDSS) test.

You give the tool the time history of one or more CDSS tests: shear stress, vertical effective stress and shear strain. For every loading cycle, it computes the test metrics that describe the shape of the stress–strain loops, the pore pressure build-up and the strain accumulation, and estimates the cyclic behavior type (CBT), a number from 0 (clay-like) to 1 (sand-like), with its uncertainty.

Why CBT

Fine-grained soils span a continuum between two kinds of cyclic response. Sand-like soils build up excess pore pressure until the effective stress nearly vanishes, and their stress–strain loops become strongly pinched (banana-shaped) as the specimen softens and re-stiffens within each cycle. Clay-like soils typically accumulate strain more gradually, keep a meaningful effective stress, and their loops stay broad. Which of these a soil follows decides how its susceptibility to cyclic softening or liquefaction should be evaluated.

Index properties such as the plasticity index separate the two only approximately. The CBT Tool instead reads the behavior from the laboratory test itself, using models fitted to the judgment of ten experienced analysts who classified 195 CDSS tests from the Next Generation Liquefaction (NGL) database.

What it computes

  • Test metrics for every cycle: the maximum excess pore pressure ratio ru,max, the loop-to-hull area ratio Ratiohull, the cyclic strain increment ratio RatioΔγ, and, for the whole record, the strain accumulation curvature κγ. See Metrics and CBT models.
  • CBT for every cycle from each of the ten analyst models, and the combined estimate: median, mean, 16th–84th and 5th–95th percentiles, and the within- and between-analyst parts of the uncertainty.
  • Plots of the test record (stress–strain loops, stress path, strain and pore pressure histories, strain accumulation), of the metrics against cycle number, and of CBT against strain.
  • Downloads of the per-cycle results and a summary table for a batch of tests, as CSV files.

Your data stays on your computer

All calculations run inside your web browser, with Python (NumPy and SciPy) compiled to WebAssembly by Pyodide. Uploaded files are not sent to any server. The first visit downloads about 30 MB, which the browser then keeps in its cache.

Where the models apply

The models were calibrated on stress-controlled CDSS tests on intact fine-grained specimens (fines content above 30%) with no initial static shear stress, carried to a double-amplitude shear strain γDA of at least 9%. The tool reports CBT for any test, but tests that stop before γDA = 9% are flagged with a caution, and use outside these conditions requires judgment. CBT estimates do not replace engineering judgment.
